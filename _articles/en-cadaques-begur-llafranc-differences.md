"Costa Brava" isn't one place — it's dozens of villages with very different personalities. For a first-time visitor, the difference between Cadaqués, Begur and Llafranc might not be obvious from the photos. For anyone about to buy there, that difference is everything.

## Cadaqués: the most distinctive, the most removed

Cadaqués holds a place of its own in the imagination of the Costa Brava — Dalí's village, white and luminous, at the end of a winding road that for decades kept it relatively cut off from the rest of the coast. That same road, today, is still part of its character: Cadaqués isn't somewhere you pass through, it's a destination in its own right.

That distinctiveness comes at a price, literally and figuratively. It's among the most expensive areas per square metre on the entire Costa Brava, and available stock is limited — many properties have stayed in the same family for generations. The typical Cadaqués buyer is after exactly that: exclusivity, genuine Mediterranean architectural heritage, and the distance from everywhere else as a feature, not a drawback.

## Begur: the balance between village and beach

Begur combines a village centre with real charm — cobbled streets, the castle up on the hill — with access to several of the most photographed coves on the Costa Brava: Sa Tuna, Aiguablava, Fornells. It's probably the area that best balances village life with proximity to the beach, without the remoteness of Cadaqués.

Begur draws a wide range of buyers: British and Dutch families after a villa with a pool in the developments on the outskirts, and buyers who prefer the historic centre for its character. The market here is more liquid than in Cadaqués — more stock, more transactions, more options across different budget ranges.

## Llafranc and Calella de Palafrugell: the classic seafront

Llafranc and its neighbour Calella de Palafrugell represent the most classic postcard image of the Costa Brava: a seafront promenade with colourful houses, small coves, and an atmosphere that still carries something of a fishing-village feel despite having been, for decades, a well-established destination.

Unlike Begur, life here is concentrated much closer to the sea — the typical property sought is a flat or house with views of the promenade or very close to it, rather than a villa with a large plot on the outskirts. It's a particularly popular area with anyone who prioritises walking to the beach and the village over having their own garden.

## So, which one should you choose?

There's no single answer, but there are questions that help:

- **After genuine remoteness and distinctiveness, and distance doesn't bother you?** Cadaqués.
- **Want a villa with a pool, close to several different coves, with good village life?** Begur.
- **Prioritise walking to the sea and a traditional seafront atmosphere?** Llafranc or Calella de Palafrugell.
- **None of the three quite fits, and what you're after is more rural, with land and quiet?** It's worth looking at the [inland Empordà]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/buying-farmhouse-emporda-renovate-guide/), just fifteen minutes from these same villages.

## A nuance almost nobody asks about at first, and should

Beyond a village's character, it's worth asking about life outside the summer months. Begur and its surroundings stay considerably more active year-round than Llafranc or Cadaqués, whose pace slows noticeably between October and May. If your plan includes spending long stretches outside summer, this nuance can matter more than any difference in price.
