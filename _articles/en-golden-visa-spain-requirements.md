Few terms have caused as much confusion among international buyers as "Golden Visa". For years, it was the best-known route to Spanish residency through buying a home. But the regulatory landscape has changed, and it's worth going into the conversation with current information, not what circulated on forums a couple of years ago.

## What the property-based Golden Visa was

The scheme, in place since 2013, allowed non-EU citizens to obtain a Spanish residence permit through a property investment of at least €500,000, without needing to actually live in the country for most of the year. It was especially popular among non-EU investors seeking free movement across the Schengen area alongside a tangible investment.

## What's changed

Spain has removed the property-investment route to the Golden Visa. Those who already held the permit before the change keep their rights under the transitional arrangements, but it's no longer possible to start a new application through this route. If your interest in buying was specifically tied to this programme, it's essential to confirm the current regulatory position with an immigration lawyer at the exact time of your enquiry — this is an area that's seen significant change, and it's worth not relying on information that's circulating out of date.

## What residency options remain open

With the property-investment route gone, other paths to Spanish residency remain available and may suit your situation:

- **Non-lucrative visa**: designed for those who can show sufficient financial means to live in Spain without needing to work here. It's the route most commonly explored by buyers looking to settle permanently without tying residency to a specific investment.
- **Digital nomad visa**: aimed at professionals working remotely for companies or clients outside Spain. It's grown notably popular among buyers combining remote work with life on the Catalan coast.
- **Family reunification**: if a close family member already holds legal residency in Spain, a reunification route may exist, with requirements specific to the relationship.
- **Other routes tied to economic or professional activity**: entrepreneurship, employment with a prior job offer, or investment in asset types other than property, each with its own set of requirements.

None of these routes requires buying a home as a condition, though nothing stops you combining them with buying a second home or a future permanent base.

## If you're a citizen of the European Union

If you hold the nationality of an EU country, the European Economic Area, or Switzerland, you don't need any visa to live in Spain: freedom of movement and establishment already covers you, with far simpler administrative steps (mainly registering as an EU resident). This is one reason why, in practice, a French, German or Dutch buyer manages settling in Spain very differently — and considerably more simply — than a buyer from outside the EU.

## The most common mistake we see

The most frequent mistake isn't failing to know that the property-based Golden Visa disappeared — an increasing number of buyers are aware of that — but assuming that, because that route no longer exists, buying a home in Spain no longer has any connection to residency at all. That's not the case: it's still entirely possible to live in Spain through other routes, and the home remains, for anyone who ultimately settles here, the centre of that life project. What's changed is that residency and buying are no longer automatically linked through a single investment programme.
