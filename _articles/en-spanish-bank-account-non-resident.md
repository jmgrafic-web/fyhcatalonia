Buying a home without opening a bank account in Spain is possible, but almost nobody does it: property tax, community fees and utility bills are far easier to pay from a local account. This guide explains how to open one and what to look at.

## What a Spanish account is for

- **Direct debits:** property tax, community fees, electricity, water, internet and insurance.
- **Paying for the purchase:** significant payments are identified in the deed, and using a Spanish account makes the bank draft and the transfer easier, as we detail in [the notary and completion day]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/spanish-notary-completion-day/).
- **Receiving financing,** if you take out a mortgage; we explain it in [mortgages for foreign buyers]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/mortgage-financing-foreigners-spain/).
- **Paying taxes** and handling the home's recurring costs.

## What banks ask a non-resident for

- A valid passport and [NIE]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/nie-number-buying-property-spain-step-by-step/).
- Proof of address in your country, such as a recent utility bill.
- In many cases, information on the source of funds or your income, and the reason for opening the account.

Requirements vary from bank to bank. Some let you open the account remotely, by video call or with a representative's help; others require you to go to a branch. Don't assume just any bank will do: ask first.

## What's worth comparing

- **Maintenance and card fees.** Non-resident accounts often have their own terms.
- **International transfers and currency exchange.** If you're sending pounds, dollars or another currency, compare the rate the bank applies with that of a currency specialist.
- **Support in your language and online banking.** It makes the difference when you manage everything from another country.
- **Deposit guarantee.** In Spain, the Deposit Guarantee Fund protects each holder's deposits up to €100,000 per institution.

## Non-resident account and resident account

Banks classify accounts according to your tax situation. If you later settle in Spain, you must tell the bank so it can update your status. Banks report foreigners' accounts to the tax authorities under international agreements, so always declare your situation honestly.

## When to open it

Ideally, open the account before you make an offer, right after getting your NIE, because the process can drag on if the bank asks for additional documents.
