A well-budgeted renovation can fail for a reason that isn't in the budget: the permit. Knowing in advance which permit your works need, and how long it takes, is part of deciding whether a property is right for you.

## Minor works and major works

The detailed rules and criteria are set by each town hall, but the general distinction is this:

- **Minor works:** simple jobs that don't affect the structure, the volume or the facade, such as renewing finishes, kitchens or bathrooms without changing the layout. Depending on the municipality, they need a minor works licence or a prior notification.
- **Major works:** jobs that affect the structure, extend the floor area, change the facade or the volume, change the use or involve a significant redistribution. They require a technical project signed by an architect, a major works licence and professional site management.

A new pool usually needs a licence, and so does demolition. When in doubt, the right answer is to ask the town hall, not to assume.

## What you usually need

- **Minor works:** a description of the works, a quote and, sometimes, simple plans.
- **Major works:** a technical project, site management, a health and safety study and the contractor's details.
- **In both cases:** payment of the municipal fees and the ICIO, the tax on construction, installations and works.

## How long it takes

It varies greatly from one municipality to another and with the complexity. As a guide, minor works are resolved in weeks and major works can take months. This time is added to that of the build itself, and it's the one that most often comes as a surprise, as we explain in [how much it costs to renovate a house on the Catalan coast]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/renovation-cost-catalan-coast-guide/).

## Rural land and farmhouses

If the house is on non-developable land, as many farmhouses in the Empordà are, the rules are stricter. Restoring a farmhouse usually requires it to be listed in the municipality's catalogue of farmhouses and rural houses, and may need reports or authorisations from the regional authorities on top of the municipal licence. It's another reason to check the planning status before you make an offer, as we explain in [buying a farmhouse in the Empordà to renovate]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/buying-farmhouse-emporda-renovate-guide/).

## What happens if you build without a licence

There can be a stop order, penalties and, in the worst case, an obligation to undo the work. An unlegalised job also complicates a sale and getting the [habitability certificate]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/habitability-certificate-catalonia/). When the work can be legalised, it can be regularised afterwards, but at more cost and with more paperwork than doing it properly from the start.

## The steps, in order

1. **Planning enquiry:** what the planning rules allow for that plot and that building.
2. **Technical project,** if the works are major.
3. **Licence application** and the town hall technicians' response.
4. **Payment of fees and ICIO** and grant of the licence.
5. **Works and supervision.**
6. **Completion of works** and updating the paperwork: cadastre, land registry and, where relevant, the certificate.
