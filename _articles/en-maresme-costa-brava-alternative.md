For years, the Maresme has been the coast of the people of Barcelona: the one you ride out to by train on a Sunday and that few outsiders really know. Today it's one of the areas most worth a look if you want the sea without giving up Barcelona.

## What the Maresme is (and isn't)

It's the stretch of coast north of Barcelona, with towns such as Caldes d'Estrac, Arenys de Mar, Vilassar de Mar, Canet de Mar or Sant Vicenç de Montalt. These are towns with a life of their own, not tourist developments: they have their shops, their market and their residents all year round. Behind them, the coastal range offers hills with sea views, and in towns like Canet de Mar you'll find modernist architecture.

It isn't the Costa Brava of hidden coves and remote villages. It's a different proposition, more everyday and better connected, and our comparison of [Sitges or the Costa Brava]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/sitges-or-costa-brava-comparison/) shows the differences in distance clearly.

## Proximity: the big argument

The R1 commuter line runs along the coast to Barcelona and the C-32 motorway runs alongside. Depending on the town, central Barcelona is between half an hour and an hour away, and El Prat airport is a reasonable drive. If you work in a hybrid pattern, have family in Barcelona or travel often, that closeness outweighs almost every other factor.

## Property types and prices

Houses with gardens and villas predominate, alongside apartments, and on the hillsides of towns like Sant Vicenç de Montalt or Caldes d'Estrac there are homes with sea views. The usual purchase range sits between €280,000 and €700,000, below what's typically asked in the most sought-after parts of the Costa Brava. The most common buyer profile is French, Dutch and German.

## Living there year-round

It's probably its greatest strength: here, life from October to May looks a lot like July and August. There are shops open, schools, healthcare and a cultural offer within easy reach. If your plan is to move rather than just holiday, it's an area worth considering alongside what we cover in [living year-round on the Costa Brava]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/living-year-round-costa-brava/).

## What to look at before buying

The Maresme has nuances that don't show in photos:

- **The train and the road.** Along much of the coast, the railway runs between the town and the beach. There are crossings, but it's worth checking how access to the sea works and how much noise reaches each specific home.
- **Hillside homes.** Retaining walls, drainage and foundations deserve a specific technical look. It's exactly what a [technical due diligence]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/technical-due-diligence-property-explained/) picks up.
- **Holiday letting.** Every municipality has its own licensing policy; if you plan to let, check it before buying, as we explain in [holiday letting and licensing in Catalonia]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/vacation-rental-catalonia-licence/).

## Who it suits, and who it doesn't

It suits you if you want to be close to Barcelona, live in a real town and enjoy the sea without a long journey every time. It suits less if what you want is the seclusion and small coves of villages like those we compare in [Cadaqués, Begur or Llafranc]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/cadaques-begur-llafranc-differences/).
