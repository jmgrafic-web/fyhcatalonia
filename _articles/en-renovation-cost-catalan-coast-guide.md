It's the question that comes up most often right after "which area should I buy in?": how much is the renovation going to cost me? The honest answer is that it depends on a lot of variables — size, structural condition, finish quality, whether the roof needs work — but it is possible to give indicative ranges that help you plan a budget from the very start, before you've even made an offer.

## Why you need a figure before you buy, not after

The most expensive mistake in a renovation usually isn't a cost overrun during the build — it's buying a property without first working out what it would cost to bring it up to the standard you want. A house with an attractive asking price can stop being a bargain the moment a poorly budgeted full renovation gets added to it.

That's why, in any serious [technical assessment]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/technical-due-diligence-property-explained/), the renovation estimate is presented across three levels — essential, considered and premium — rather than as a single figure. This lets you compare properties realistically: one with a higher asking price but in good condition can work out cheaper overall than a less expensive one that needs full structural work.

## The three renovation levels

**Essential.** Updated installations (electrics, plumbing), paint, flooring and functional bathrooms, with no major structural changes. The right level when the property is in reasonably good general condition and just needs bringing up to date.

**Considered.** Includes a partial reconfiguration of the layout, a fully new kitchen and bathrooms, improved insulation and, in many cases, a roof upgrade or work on installations that the essential level would leave untouched. It's the most common level across the typical purchase on the Catalan coast.

**Premium.** Structural work where needed, a full reconfiguration, high-end materials, full climate control and, often, extending the usable floor area. It's the typical level for a farmhouse being fully restored, or a villa where the result shouldn't involve any compromise.

## What pushes the budget up

A handful of factors push up the cost of a renovation significantly, whatever finish level you choose:

- **Roof or structural work.** If the timber roof structure needs touching, or load-bearing walls need reinforcing, the cost jumps noticeably — and it's exactly what a technical due diligence picks up before you buy, not after.
- **Age of the installations.** A property with electrics or plumbing over thirty years old almost always needs full replacement, not just an update.
- **Site access.** Farmhouses or houses in historic centres with difficult access for machinery and materials make the logistics of the build more expensive.
- **Outstanding legalisation.** If there are extensions or past changes without a licence, they may need legalising before work can continue, adding time and professional fees.
- **Kitchen and bathroom finish level.** These two rooms almost always account for the biggest share of the difference in budget between a considered and a premium finish.

## Realistic timelines

A full renovation on the Catalan coast typically runs between 4 and 8 months from signing with the contractor, depending on scope. A full restoration of a [farmhouse]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/buying-farmhouse-emporda-renovate-guide/), or work requiring an architectural project and a major building licence, can run longer than that because of council processing times, more than the build itself.

Any quote that doesn't include an estimated timeline, or that promises one notably shorter than these figures without explaining why, is worth a second look.

## How to avoid budget surprises during the build

The most effective way to keep a budget on track is to have, from the start, an objective comparison between several contractors — not just on price, but on timeline, communication style and proven track record — and active supervision during the build, with regular reports and milestone sign-off by phase. A budget nobody oversees tends to grow; one that's reviewed stage by stage usually doesn't.
