It's one of the questions we hear most from international buyers who don't yet know the area well: Sitges or Costa Brava? The right answer doesn't come down to price or how the photos look, but to the kind of life you picture living there.

## The fundamental difference

[Sitges]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/sitges-buying-guide-cosmopolitan-area/) is, above all, proximity with an urban lifestyle. Under forty minutes from Barcelona and twenty from the airport, it combines beach, culture, dining and a genuinely established international community — historically popular, in fact, with British and American buyers. It's the natural choice for anyone who wants a second home without giving up on city life, or for anyone planning to live there full-time without feeling cut off.

The Costa Brava, by contrast, is distance and character. Its key towns — Begur, Pals, Llafranc, [Cadaqués]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/cadaques-begur-llafranc-differences/), Tamariu — sit an hour and a half to two hours from Barcelona, and offer something Sitges no longer can: coves that still feel uncrowded, untouched Mediterranean architecture, and a markedly slower pace of life, especially outside the summer months.

## Type of property

In Sitges, the market revolves mainly around modernist townhouses, penthouses and premium apartments, with prices typically ranging between €350,000 and €900,000. It's a more urban market in character, though with real identity thanks to the late-19th-century architecture.

On the Costa Brava, the range is wider — between €400,000 and €1.2 million — and the dominant property type is the villa, the renovated farmhouse, or the house with a garden, often with sea views or close to a cove. If what you're after is a plot with space, your own pool and some distance from the neighbours, the Costa Brava delivers that far more naturally than Sitges.

## Connectivity and day-to-day life

This point often matters more than any other. If your plan involves travelling frequently between your home country and Spain, or hosting visitors regularly, Sitges's proximity to Barcelona-El Prat airport is an advantage that's hard to match. If, instead, the second home is for long stays and genuine disconnection, the Costa Brava's distance stops being a drawback and becomes part of the appeal.

Life outside the season matters too. Sitges stays busy and well-served all year round, thanks to its size and proximity to Barcelona. Many Costa Brava villages, by contrast, change pace noticeably in winter — something some buyers value as peace and quiet, and others experience as isolation.

## The typical buyer in each area

It's no coincidence that we see different patterns depending on the area. In Sitges, the buyer tends to be British, Dutch or American, often combining a second home with the possibility of relocating later, and someone who values staying socially active. On the Costa Brava, alongside the British and Dutch buyer, there's a stronger French presence — thanks to the proximity via the AP-7 motorway — and more interest in the renovation project and the landscape than in urban life.

## A third option: the inland Empordà

If neither of the two quite fits — if what you're after is neither the coastal life of Sitges nor the seafront of the Costa Brava, but something more rural with more character of its own — it's worth looking at the inland Empordà: farmhouses, village houses and estates away from the coast, generally with more accessible budgets.
