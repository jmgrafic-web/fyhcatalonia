The NIE is the first document any foreign buyer needs, and the one that delays the most purchases when it's left until the end. This guide explains what it is, how to get it and how to avoid the most common bottlenecks.

## What the NIE is (and isn't)

The NIE (foreigner identification number) is the number the Spanish authorities use to identify foreigners for tax and administrative purposes. It has a recognisable format — a letter (X, Y or Z), seven digits and another letter — and it's personal and for life: one person, one NIE.

It is not a residence permit and it doesn't make you a tax resident. It's simply an identification number. You'll need it to buy the property, open a bank account, connect utilities and pay or declare taxes.

## The three ways to get one

1. **In Spain, in person.** At a foreigners' office or a National Police station that handles applications, by appointment. You submit form EX-15, your passport, the reason for the application (for a purchase, an offer or reservation contract usually does) and payment of the fee (form 790, code 012), which is a few euros.
2. **At the Spanish consulate in your country.** Useful if you aren't travelling yet. Each consulate has its own requirements and timings, so check first.
3. **Through a representative.** Your lawyer or administrator, with a power of attorney, can handle it for you. It's the most convenient route when appointments are scarce or when you won't be coming over until completion.

## Usual documents

- A valid passport, and a copy.
- A completed EX-15 form.
- A document showing the reason for the application: for example, the purchase offer or the reservation contract.
- Proof of payment of the fee.

Exact requirements vary by office, so confirm the list before you go.

## How long it takes

It depends on the route and the province. In person, once you have the appointment, the certificate is usually issued the same day or within a few days; through a consulate it can take longer. What delays things most isn't the procedure itself but the appointment, which is hard to get in some provinces. Book early.

## The mistakes that delay a purchase most

- **Leaving it until after the offer is accepted.** Once the offer is accepted the clock is running, and the seller's deadlines won't wait for your appointment.
- **Applying for a second NIE.** If you've had one before — from working or studying here — it can be recovered with your passport; you don't apply for another.
- **Confusing it with the EU citizen registration certificate.** That's a different procedure, the residence registration for EU citizens, and it doesn't replace the NIE for buying.
- **Giving a generic reason.** Give the specific transaction, not a vague explanation.
