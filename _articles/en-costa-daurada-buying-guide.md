The Costa Daurada is the Catalan coast that most often gets left out of international buyers' first searches and, for many profiles, the one that offers the best balance of price and quality of life. This guide explains what's really there.

## What it covers

It's the coast of the province of Tarragona, south of Barcelona, with towns such as Cambrils, Salou, Miami Platja, L'Hospitalet de l'Infant, Torredembarra, Altafulla and the city of Tarragona itself. Its name comes from the long golden-sand beaches. Sitges, which is sometimes associated with this area, actually belongs to the Garraf coast and deserves a guide of its own: [Sitges, the most cosmopolitan area]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/sitges-buying-guide-cosmopolitan-area/).

## A territory with several characters

- **Tarragona city:** a capital whose Roman heritage is a UNESCO World Heritage site, with year-round urban life and a full range of services.
- **Cambrils:** a fishing port with a reputation for its food and a seafaring-town feel.
- **Altafulla and Torredembarra:** towns with an old quarter and a sandy beach, quieter and more residential.
- **Salou and Miami Platja:** areas more geared to tourism, with plenty of apartments and activity strongly shaped by the season.

## Property types and prices

Apartments, villas and townhouses predominate. The usual purchase range sits between €160,000 and €450,000, the most accessible entry point of the areas where we work. Among buyers, Dutch, Latin American and French are common.

## Connections

The high-speed station at Camp de Tarragona links to Barcelona in around half an hour, and the AP-7 motorway runs the length of the coast. Reus airport has some flights and Barcelona-El Prat is roughly an hour's drive from most of the area.

## What to watch for

- **Seasonality.** In the more tourist-oriented areas, life changes a lot between summer and winter. Visit them off-season before deciding; we cover it in [living year-round on the Costa Brava]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/living-year-round-costa-brava/), with criteria that apply here just as well.
- **The condition of the building and the community.** Many apartments are in buildings that are decades old. Ask for the latest owners' meeting minutes and check for outstanding special levies, and rely on a [technical due diligence]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/technical-due-diligence-property-explained/) for the facade, roof and installations.
- **Holiday letting.** The area invites you to run the numbers, and it's worth doing so calmly: the licence first, described in [holiday letting in Catalonia]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/vacation-rental-catalonia-licence/), and then the real figure, as we show in [buy-to-let profitability]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/blog/buy-to-let-profitability-catalan-coast/).
