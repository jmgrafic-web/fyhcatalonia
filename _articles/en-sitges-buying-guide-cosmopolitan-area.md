Of all the areas we work in, Sitges is probably the one that needs the least introduction — and, at the same time, the one that carries the most misconceptions among buyers who only know it in passing. This guide is written for anyone seriously considering buying here, not just visiting for a weekend.

## Why Sitges is different from the rest of the coast

Under forty minutes from Barcelona and twenty from El Prat airport, Sitges combines something no other area of the Catalan coast offers to the same degree: genuine urban life — dining, culture, a long-established international community — without losing its essence as a coastal town, with central beaches and an old quarter of narrow streets and whitewashed houses.

That combination explains why Sitges has historically attracted a different buyer profile from the Costa Brava: more oriented towards social life and connectivity, less towards isolation or rural living.

## The type of property you'll find

The Sitges market mainly moves across three property types:

- **Modernist houses in the old quarter**: the most sought-after and most limited property type, with facades from the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Buying here almost always means taking on a renovation project that respects protected original features.
- **Seafront penthouses and apartments**: the most liquid part of the market, with a wide price range depending on exact proximity to the promenade and the building's condition.
- **Villas in the outlying developments**: towards areas like Terramar or the hills surrounding the town centre, with their own plot, pool and more privacy — in exchange for losing walkability to the centre.

## What surprises buyers comparing it with the Costa Brava

A buyer who's already looked at the Costa Brava usually notices two differences straight away in Sitges: beachfront prices per square metre are generally higher, given the proximity to Barcelona and more limited land availability; and the market is considerably more liquid — properties change hands more often, which also means more competition among buyers for the best-located properties.

## Life outside the season: the big difference

Unlike much of the Costa Brava, Sitges stays genuinely active all year round. Its proximity to Barcelona and its own population size mean restaurants, shops and social life don't depend on the tourist season the same way. It's probably the factor that weighs most for those choosing Sitges over other areas: here, life from October to May looks fairly similar to July and August — something that doesn't hold true for most Costa Brava villages.

## A heritage nuance worth knowing

Much of Sitges's architectural charm — the modernist houses of the old quarter — carries some degree of municipal heritage protection, meaning any renovation must respect certain original elements: facade, massing, sometimes catalogued interior features. This doesn't prevent renovation, but it does shape the type of project and licensing timeline, which is worth knowing before you buy, not when you submit the renovation project.
